How does it work?

The solver uses the subgroup chain G ⊃ H = ⟨U, D, R2, L2, F2, B2⟩. Phase 1 brings the cube into H (corner twist, edge flip and middle-slice edges in their slice: the three coordinates of the quotient space G/H, 2187 × 2048 × 495 cosets). Phase 2 solves inside H, which has only 19,508,428,800 elements. Both phases use IDA* with pruning tables built by BFS over the coordinates.

Theory in one minute

What is what
  • Actions: each turn is a permutation of 8 corners and 12 edges plus orientation changes. A state is the action that produces it from the solved cube.
  • Product: A·B = do A, then B. It is associative, has an identity (do nothing) and inverses (R⁻¹ = R'), but it is not commutative.
  • Order of an element: how many times it must be repeated to get back to the start. A cycle of length L with zero twist contributes L; with non-zero twist it contributes 3L (corners) or 2L (edges). The order is the lcm of everything. R U has order 105.
Why |G| = 43,252,003,274,489,856,000

G ≅ (Z₃⁷ × Z₂¹¹) ⋊ ((A₈ × A₁₂) ⋊ Z₂). Start from the free space of 8!·3⁸·12!·2¹² configurations and impose three independent constraints: the corner twists sum to 0 (mod 3) → ÷3, the edge flips sum to 0 (mod 2) → ÷2, and the corner permutation parity equals the edge permutation parity → ÷2. Total: 8!·12!·3⁷·2¹¹ / 2. These constraints are invariants: no legal turn can break them (check them in the state panel).
